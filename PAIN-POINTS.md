# What's annoying about stock Swapcard

A specific list of the friction in Swapcard's attendee web app (app.swapcard.com), written as the steps you actually take. Observed at EA Global: New York City 2026 in October 2026. Each item ends with what Calmer Card does about it.

## Deciding whether to accept a meeting request

**You want to:** decide whether to accept a 1:1 request from someone you don't know.

1. A red counter appears on the bell icon. You click the bell.
2. A drawer opens with "Connection requests", "Meeting requests" and "Activities". The request shows a name, job title, organization, and the first line of their message.
3. That isn't enough to judge them, so you click their name. The drawer closes and you land on their full profile page.
4. "About me" is cut off after three lines. You click "See more".
5. Their goals ("What am I hoping to get out of the event") and "How I can help others" are further down, so you scroll.
6. Their LinkedIn is at the very bottom, under "Social media". You scroll to the bottom and click it.
7. You still don't know if the proposed time works, because the request says "Sat 5:00 PM" and nothing about your Saturday. You open My Schedule (new page) and scroll to Saturday 5 PM.
8. You go back to the bell (or the profile) to click Accept. The drawer has reset; you find the request again.

**Why it hurts:** about eight clicks and three page loads per request, and you lose your place each time. At EAG you get dozens.

**Calmer Card:** the Inbox lists the request with "Meeting · Sat 5:00 PM · free/clash". Selecting it pins a card above the chat showing your day 90 minutes either side (with rooms) and the proposed slot in place, plus Accept / Decline / Suggest another time. The full profile (LinkedIn on top, full bio, goals, "how I can help") sits in the right column. Keyboard: A to accept, D to decline.

## Requests and messages live in two places

**You want to:** catch up on everything people sent you.

1. Meeting and connection requests are in the bell drawer.
2. Chat messages are behind the speech-bubble icon, on the Messages page.
3. When someone sends a meeting request with a message, the request is in the bell and the message is in Messages. You read one, then go to the other to reply.
4. Nothing tells you which items still need you. Read and unread, accepted and pending look the same in Messages.

**Calmer Card:** one Inbox with one row per person, requests and chats merged. People who need you (incoming requests, unread messages) come first, then "waiting on them", then the rest. A "Needs you (n)" filter and search. The bell and chat icons are gone; one badge on Inbox counts both.

## Booking a 1:1 with someone

**You want to:** find a time to meet someone you found in Attendees.

1. You open their profile (full page) and click Meet.
2. The modal shows four time chips for the first day only ("Fri 4:00 PM, 5:00 PM...").
3. You can't see your own day here, so you don't know which of these clash with a session you meant to attend, or leave no break between meetings. You close the modal, open My Schedule, look, come back, and reopen Meet.
4. To see other days you click "See more slots": a second dialog with columns of chips per day. Still no view of your schedule.
5. You pick a chip. A third dialog asks for a location. Then a description. Then "Send meeting request".

**Calmer Card:** inside Swapcard's own Meet modal, a calendar of your day (your meetings and sessions) with the times you're both free highlighted; slots that overlap something you're attending are marked. Picking one selects Swapcard's matching slot, so their flow continues. From any profile side panel, "Book a 1:1" does the same thing without leaving the page.

## Choosing which sessions to go to

**You want to:** pick what to attend on Saturday afternoon.

1. You open Agenda. It's a long list of large cards, about five per screen, one day at a time.
2. Your own meetings aren't on the Agenda. To check if a 2:30 talk clashes with your 2:00 meeting, you switch to My Schedule.
3. My Schedule's calendar shows only what you've already added. The sessions you're deciding between aren't there.
4. So you flip between Agenda (what exists) and My Schedule (what you have), comparing times in your head.
5. Clicking a session opens a full page with a big placeholder banner. Back returns you to the top of the Agenda list.

**Calmer Card:** one Schedule tab: the calendar, with every session you're not attending drawn in grey at its real time. Hover for details, click for a side panel (description, seats, speakers, who's registered, Add/Remove). Your own blocks keep the left side of each day; grey sessions take the right.

## Too many sessions at the same time

**You want to:** see what's on at 2 PM Saturday, when ten things run at once.

1. In Agenda you scroll through ten full cards to compare them.
2. There's no way to say "not interested" in a session, so the same twenty Office Hours show up every time you look.
3. Sessions that already ended stay on the list.

**Calmer Card:** busy times collapse into one "N other sessions" block; clicking it opens a full-width day view where each session is a real block side by side. ✕ hides a session ("Not for me"), "Hide all 'Office hours' (19)" hides a whole family, with Undo, and a "Show hidden" switch brings them back. Sessions that have ended drop off, and a red line marks now.

## Scanning attendees

**You want to:** find interesting people to meet.

1. Attendees shows about five people per screen: photo, name, title, organization. No interests, no goals.
2. Above the list, an "Online now" carousel and a "Need to meet? / +8550 meeting requests already sent!" box take space.
3. To learn anything about a person you click them: full page load.
4. You press Back. The list reloads at the top, and you scroll down to find your place again.

**Calmer Card:** the carousel and promo box are gone. Clicking a person opens a side panel over the list (LinkedIn first, full bio, goals, "how I can help", interests as chips), so you never lose your place. Clicking someone inside a panel replaces it, with Back; Cmd-click still opens a new tab.

## Checking a meeting you already have

**You want to:** confirm where your 5 PM is, or tell them you're running late.

1. You click the meeting on your calendar. It opens Swapcard's meeting page (full page) with a floating chat popup.
2. To see who the person is you click "Open profile": another page.
3. To get back to your calendar you press Back twice.

**Calmer Card:** clicking a meeting opens a side panel over the calendar: confirmed / waiting / they proposed, time and room, the right actions (Accept-Decline, Cancel request, or Reschedule and Cancel with a confirm click), the latest message with "Open conversation", and their profile.

## The calendar itself

- **It opens at midnight.** You scroll down to 8 AM every time. *(Calmer Card opens at 8 AM.)*
- **List view is the default.** You click "Calendar" every time. *(Always the calendar.)*
- **Content is capped at about 1260px** with a sidebar (Appearance, a colour legend, time zone) taking a column. *(Full width; options live in a small "View" button.)*
- **Text gets cut mid-line** in short blocks, and back-to-back blocks touch. *(Lines drop cleanly, small gaps.)*
- **Venue logistics** (venue open, badge pickup) are drawn as hours-long blocks over the day. *(One quiet line under each date.)*
- **You can't leave a session from the calendar.** You open it and find "Remove from schedule". *(✕ on hover.)*

## Navigation

- **Home, My Schedule and Agenda overlap.** Home is a list of links that repeats the nav, with Wi-Fi and the code of conduct at the bottom. *(One "Schedule" tab; Home and Agenda redirect to it.)*
- **Two header rows** (logo and account, then the event nav) take vertical space. *(One row when the window is wide enough.)*
- **The bell and the chat icon** each lead to half of your inbox. *(Removed; Inbox covers both.)*
- **A "My profile / My contacts / My schedule / My bookmarks" picker** sits above the calendar, and the avatar menu repeats the same shortcuts. *(Both removed.)*
- **The current tab isn't obvious** in the nav. *(Highlighted with an underline.)*
- **The EAG Matcher** is a separate site in a new tab. *(Unchanged.)*
